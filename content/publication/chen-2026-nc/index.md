abstract: "The S = 1/2 Heisenberg antiferromagnetic chain is a paradigmatic quantum system hosting exotic excitations. Using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) on copper benzoate, we characterise three distinct types of excitations by tuning the magnetic field at ultra-low temperatures. At 0.2 T, a nearly constant spin-lattice relaxation rate ($1/T_1$) and a divergence-like spin-spin relaxation rate ($1/T_{2G}$) upon cooling provide experimental evidence for spinon excitations. Below 0.4 K, an additional relaxation channel, characterised by $1/T_1 \\propto T$ with spectral weight growing as $-\\ln(T/T_0)$ and a divergence of the NMR Knight shift ($K_n$), reveals random-singlet-like behaviours. Above 1 T, a field-induced spin gap $\\Delta \\propto H^{2/3}$ signifies the onset of soliton excitations. This work offers NMR identification of spinons, solitons, and the low-energy behaviours of a random-singlet-like state in copper benzoate, and establishes this material as a unique platform for studying the interplay of quantum integrability and disorder."
